# Shared-host topology: strict training-only reference (2026-11-24)

Exploratory extension of PR #153, using original 454 H7-period observations, 222 paired H7/89 H6, quality 173/77. No new field observations or actual feeding, competition, abundance, biomass, nitrogen transfer, or wastewater effects. Six original archive hashes, exact original 499-permutation source network, original H6 groups and quality roster revalidated by the preserved source script. Compare 36 initial plus 44 nested endpoint/cohort/consumer specifications, each with baseline and added-EQR heldout models. Both consumer definitions (fixed early and symmetric) retained; symmetric uses late consumer records to define the outcome, not as a predictor. The original H6 folds, H6-equal Ridge10 and two model implementations are maintained. Bootstrap intervals describe fixed parent-heldout losses; not full parameter uncertainty or independent replication. No country-transfer or measured-TP replication performed for these new endpoints.

## Fixed host source and exact rarefaction
The original catalogue has 52 of 75 Lycaenidae keys with resolved hosts. Among all 990 candidate host keys across the five butterfly families, 211 have at least one Lycaenidae catalogue edge: 178 degree 1, 16 degree 2, 9 degree 3, 4 degree 4, 2 degree 5, 1 degree 6, 1 degree 8. Only 33 shared Lycaenidae host keys; 16 are Fabaceae. Degree zero means no mapped Lycaenidae edge in this catalogue, not a proven non-host; missing links and taxonomic/host reporting biases remain. 1,326 potential unordered pairs of the 52 mapped butterfly keys; sum of possible pair-by-host triples = 130 (not unique pairs, feeding events, or pairwise interactions). Joint butterfly inclusion probability in the SAME original 20-record rarefaction = p_i+p_j-p_(i union j), checked against independent hypergeometric PMF on a fixed grid. Multiply by plant 50-record inclusion probability to define expected catalogue-compatible pair-by-host triples, assuming separate record subsampling. The single-link index uses the earlier rarefaction. All values are record-based opportunities, not realized niche overlap or competition.

## Strict null and nested components
Null is exact average of column permutations within plant family × training-early occurrence-frequency bin × training-early geography cluster; all heldout parent H6 and late records excluded from null construction. Additional nested nulls hold (i) presence of at least one catalogue Lycaenidae edge, (ii) exact catalogue Lycaenidae degree. Plant change is weighted by either early-only or symmetric consumer leverage. The difference between observed and null is split into eligibility, within-eligible degree, and remaining partner identity (exact algebra; summed to original excess within 1e-12). All models use the same original baseline covariates plus early single- and pair-link excess, then add early EQR. Different outcome scales imply that paired gains are prediction comparisons, not common ecological effect sizes. Exact degree matching permits swaps for only 4–8 shared keys in quality folds, 6–10 in all folds; poor support for specific identity contrasts. Independent sklearn predictions and numerical ridge solver checked for every model. Source-only uniform pair degree diagnostic is choose(degree,2), not actual overlap.

Single-total EQR slope negative across folds. Early-fixed single total: 6.08 [-2.10,12.49] quality and 9.54 [1.51,17.24] all. Pair total early-fixed: 1.16 [-1.82,3.89] quality and 2.65 [-0.55,5.27] all. No corrected pair-total minus single-total difference supports stronger shared-host prediction. The pair sharing versus pair identity contrast in the all early-fixed sample is positive after within-specification Bonferroni-4 (+5.73 pp [0.27,18.28]); neither pair component shows clearly positive predictive gain and identity is poorly supported by movable keys. Do not interpret this as ecological competition, enhanced overlap, or a robust global mechanism. No additional nested comparison consistently supports a specific component. Preserve nulls and uncertainty: the broader plant-side compositional relationship remains, but its mechanistic attribution to resource sharing, individual partner identities, or catalogue eligibility remains unresolved. Additional direct data and genuinely independent validation are needed.
