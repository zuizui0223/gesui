# 2026-11-24: Lycaenidae resource-network decomposition and aquatic correspondence

Status: source-locked **exploratory / posthoc**, not independently collected field data, not measured feeding or sewage-derived nutrient transport. This snapshot preserves the previous conversation-derived analysis outputs byte-for-byte, without rehosting the underlying third-party source archives. Main is not changed by this draft.

## Main findings and essential counterevidence

- Original European network: 454 H7-period observations, 232 H7 and 90 H6, 303 recorded butterfly species keys, 990 catalogue plant keys, 1,565 known candidate edges. Repeated panel 222 H7/89 H6; paired quality >=80% in all source groups 173 H7/77 H6. Original network refers to GBIF-record rarefaction, not animal/plant abundance. Expected edge count uses independent inclusion at 20 butterfly records and 50 plant records; reference is mean of 499 host-identity reassignments restricted by plant family, occurrence frequency bin and six H6 occurrence clusters. Those clusters use the entire two-period dataset, so the prediction exercise is retrospective, not a genuinely prospective forecast.
- Across the quality panel, earlier benthic EQR improves prediction of later network excess by 10.02%; Lycaenidae alone 15.53%, all 222 H7 10.92%. Reverse time and same-period changes do not securely replicate the association; country and site selection are important. In the earlier analyses, common H6, baseline, and outcomes were retained for family comparisons, but those analyses are not independent field replications.
- Removal of the highest contributing Lycaenidae hosts: top 1/5/20 removal leaves EQR-associated gain 16.00/12.87/9.98% (quality panel); see original exclusion and model tables, including nonpositive comparisons. Fabaceae accounts for 51.5% of observed Lycaenidae candidate edge index and 94.6% of its signed net excess. This is not a biomass or nitrogen-fixation estimate.
- Catalogue host grouping: 18 Fabaceae-only, 7 mixed, 27 non-Fabaceae-only, 23 unknown among 75 Lycaenidae keys. Spatial net excess relative to reference +42.97/+6.46/+3.59% for the three scored groups. The mixed group has positive retrospective EQR prediction gain +9.71%, versus +1.72% and -3.91%; these point estimates are not physiological host switching or a universal specialization law.
- Exact symmetric two-period change decomposition of the Lycaenidae excess: plant-side EQR predictive gain +14.66% [7.12,22.24], butterfly-side -0.72% [-2.72,0.24]; all paired H7 plant side +10.40% [4.08,17.74]. The terms are contributions to an algebraic index, not independent causal effects. Paired H6 bootstrap contrasts are descriptive.
- Plant-side quantity/identity split: all-host quantity -2.67% versus identity +17.47%; Fabaceae/non-Fabaceae quantity +0.41% versus within-group identity +16.68%; within-family identity +9.61%. Quantity is expected recorded host-species inclusion, NOT plant biomass. NUTRI linear projection -0.91% versus residual identity +10.25%. Only 328/990 host keys have exact NUTRI (31/119 Fabaceae); strict 80% coverage fails. Residual identity is not a measured functional trait or proof of nutrient independence.
- Same physical river monitoring cohort in France (263 stations, 73 H7, 24 H6; 2018/2022) does NOT reproduce direct river TP to butterfly network, nor the original whole-panel EQR signal on the matched station subset. Plant-side model TP gain -3.19%, matched EQR +1.87% (uncertain); modelled W +8.89% (uncertain). This work does not establish a wastewater-to-land causal pathway.
