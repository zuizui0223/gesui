# 2025 Denmark eBMS: NEW399 source-lock shows compositional sign reversal, not a sewage benefit

**2026-11-10. Original licensed data, two independently written family-count pipelines and independent SHA reconciliation PASSED. Entire hypothesis family and denominator diagnostics are POSTHOC relative to the earlier Germany/Austria Lycaenidae photograph-positive result. The new 2025 site list was frozen before original NEW399 family counts, but geography/exposure are observational and no causal wastewater effect is identifiable.**

## Why this is a distinct new sample

The previous 2023–2025 repeated-site Denmark cohort included 107 eligible coordinates, and was analyzed before this phase. We excluded **all 107 site hashes** before reading the 2025 family counts for the new study. The original Denmark eBMS v1.4 (GBIF caa38f94-ddeb-43e7-9a27-1ac46d7ddc5c, **CC-BY-NC 4.0**, exact ZIP SHA256 `2cc64ec352754223db06a92eef5b01803968eac94bae34057c17f1abdb6da79b`) has 15,606 Event and 21,617 Occurrence rows, and the original blinded GBIF 112-species-name family registry was fixed before these new counts.

The same official **2022 EEA Danish registered qualifying N/P-positive freshwater WWTP outfall inventory (203 qualifying outfalls)** is a static **geographic proximity** covariate in 2025; neither actual river transport to butterfly habitats nor any 2025 measured effluent nutrients are available.

Original 2025 Event-only eligible sites: 506, excluding the old 107 gives **399 NEW sites** (100 within 5km of a registered outfall, 127 >=10km from one, 172 intermediate). Predeclared independent observation-opportunity QC requiring at least 2 distinct 2025 survey dates having ANY original published Occurrence on each site admitted **70 near, 92 remote, 124 intermediate** (286/399). Near 70 qualified site rows are mapped to **18 distinct registered source IDs**, and **17 5km spatially connected source-location components**, not 70 independent experimental source systems. Six original country×1degree geographic cells contain both near and remote with focal families recorded.

All source counts, source IDs, original Event days, observed Occurrence-linked days and accepted counts in **six butterfly families** were reconstructed independently in two original-archive pipelines. Across all **399/399 individual site hashes, there were ZERO mismatches** in source status, source IDs, 1degree location, source 5km/10km component identity, all original survey day counts, and all six accepted family individualCount values. This is a strong original aggregate integrity result, not independent biological evidence.

## Main result: same Lycaenidae records, different direction depending on response definition

| Original 2025 accepted eBMS count or response | NEW near (<=5km) | NEW remote (>=10km) | Near / remote comparison |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qualified site coordinate rows | 70 | 92 | 17 independent near WWTP spatial components |
| Original dates with ANY published Occurrence | 539 | 757 | Not certified all-biota detection-free on other Event dates |
| Lycaenidae individuals | 382 | 644 | — |
| Lycaenidae per date with a published Occurrence | **0.709** | **0.851** | **0.833× (near lower)** |
| Pieridae individuals | 680 | 2,602 | Remote strongly concentrated |
| Nymphalidae individuals | 3,822 | 3,544 | Near 1.51× per documented date |
| Hesperiidae individuals | 372 | 192 | Near 2.72× per documented date |
| Lycaenidae share of three families (Lyca + Pieridae + Papilionidae) | **36.0%** | 19.8% | **+16.13 percentage points / OR 2.270** |
| Lycaenidae share of five butterfly families (adding Nymphalidae + Hesperiidae) | **7.27%** | **9.22%** | **−1.96 percentage points / OR 0.772** |

Papilionidae = 0 in this supported cohort, so the three-family fraction effectively compares Lycaenidae versus Pieridae alone. The three-family apparent positive is therefore at least partially a relative **Pieridae decrease/increase in geographic observer reporting**; it does not mean an increase in actual Lycaenidae individuals. Replacing the denominator with five independently accepted butterfly families reverses the pooled Lycaenidae proportion sign while the **Lycaenidae absolute per published date stays lower** near registered WWTPs.

## Geographic balance and leverage tests

The original six shared 1degree cells were analyzed with **equal geographic weighting**, rather than summing all individuals into one sample. For three-family composition the equal-region geometric near/remote odds ratio is **0.908** (6 region mean logOR −0.097; positive in **2/6**). For five-family composition the equal-region near/remote odds ratio is **0.404** (mean logOR −0.905; positive in **1/6**). The actual Lycaenidae per-reported-date near/remote rate is above 1 in only **2/6** of the cells. These are six nonrandom geographic groups, not experimental replicates.

The earlier 10,000 descriptive resampling checks gave source-block bootstrap 95% logOR ranges **[−1.308, +1.313]** for the three-family local geographical contrast and **[−1.727, −0.061]** for the five-family local contrast. The latter range excludes zero in this **POSTHOC** denominator-sensitivity run but is NOT a confirmatory source effect, because denominator choice was explored after seeing the result, the number of independent 1degree groups is only six, source proximity is confounded, and no hydrological exposure was observed.

Original remotely recorded Pieridae counts are highly concentrated: **the five largest remote Pieridae site rows supply 1,321 / 2,602 = 50.8%** of Pieridae individuals. An explicitly outcome-dependent deletion of the ten largest remote Pieridae sites would reverse the original three-family pooled source odds ratio from **2.270 to 0.815**. This deletion is **not** a valid inference strategy; it demonstrates leverage and why the positive association is highly contingent on the comparator denominator.

The six-region values and WWTP source-cluster leave-one-out diagnostics are preserved in the original source-locked aggregate JSON. The additional independent same-cohort audit exactly reproduces all 399 site rows and both denominator signs, using the second source parser and separate computation.

## Original 107-site cohort does not rescue the 3-family positive

On the **previous, separately recruited** repeated-site 107-site cohort using the same 2025 records: Lycaenidae three-family proportion was **39.1% near vs 64.4% remote** (negative source proximity association); under the five-family denominator it was **7.14% near vs 14.58% remote** (also negative). The new399 three-family pooled sign is positive, while the older 107-site sign is negative **in the same 2025 year**. That conflict is real and must be reported: different site recruitment, source outfall representation and denominator composition imply that neither pooled sign is a generally transferable WWTP ecology law.

The 2025 five-family near-lower direction agrees between those two observational cohorts, but still does **NOT** prove a biochemical effluent harm. Site-level public recording completeness is heterogeneous, WWTP outfall IDs may share upstream hydrology, source status was determined by 2022 reporting, host plant/land-cover/season variation persists, and butterfly sampling is not randomized relative to source proximity.

## Scientific decision

**Confirmed new finding as an observation-methodology result:** at the same 2025 Danish eBMS standardized sites the pooled sign of Lycaenidae versus WWTP proximity changes according to whether Pieridae-only or five-family composition is the comparator. Controlling geographical aggregation or using the actual Lycaenidae count rate greatly weakens or reverses the apparent three-family positive. This is **Simpson-like aggregation/denominator dependence, not a strict Simpson paradox** because the local six-region signs are mixed.

**Not confirmed:** that wastewater increases or decreases Lycaenidae population abundance, has a direct adult mud-puddling nutritional subsidy, changes ant mutualisms, or affects larval host supply. The stronger paper angle from this dataset is the instability of opportunistic family-composition interpretations across independent monitoring designs, with geography and survey documentation explicitly audited. A mechanistic WWTP biodiversity claim still requires physical upstream/downstream receiving habitat exposure plus independent N/Na/organic substrate chemistry and many true source systems.
