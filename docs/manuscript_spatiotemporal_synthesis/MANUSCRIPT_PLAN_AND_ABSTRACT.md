# Spatial correspondence and limited temporal predictability in wastewater-associated catchment assemblages
**Integrated manuscript plan — 25 November 2026; draft, not submission.**

## Decision
The research question is whether environmental and consumer–resource associations across catchments also predict change through time, and whether a river-to-terrestrial wastewater pathway can be distinguished from persistent shared geography. The principal result is not a demonstrated causal wastewater chain or universal absence of temporal effects. It is a set of observed, source-specific discrepancies between spatial predictability and incremental temporal predictability in aquatic and terrestrial compartments. Use the same-site French river analysis as the aquatic anchor, the same-H7 France73 trait comparison as the cross-compartment anchor, and the previously frozen three-window butterfly forecast followed by the four-window change test as the terrestrial extension. The long-term EQR–Lycaenidae network link remains exploratory and catalogue-dependent.

This is an alternative synthesis stacked on PR160, not an overwrite or merge of the November 21 manuscript in PR124. No new ecological fit, data acquisition, or independent effect-size synthesis was performed for this manuscript step. No claim of a first discovery in the literature.

## Abstract (English working draft)

Environmental gradients can structure biological assemblages across space, but their ability to predict temporal change need not follow from spatial associations. We evaluated wastewater-associated geography, river chemistry, benthic ecological condition, plant habitat affinity and butterfly larval-host resources across European catchments, distinguishing spatial association, temporally held-out prediction and source attribution. At 263 repeatedly sampled French river stations in 73 catchments, measured total phosphorus improved geographically held-out prediction of benthic ecological quality in 2018 and 2022 by 30.8% and 22.7%, respectively, but phosphorus changes did not improve prediction of ecological-quality changes (−0.4%). In the same catchment frame, plant nutrient-habitat affinity predicted butterfly host-habitat affinity beyond river phosphorus (+30.2%), whereas river phosphorus added no clear predictive information about terrestrial indices. In a temporally held-out forecast of butterfly recording composition across 133 quality-filtered catchments, larval-host occurrence information provided small additional predictive gains for 46 Lycaenidae species using two host catalogues (+0.33% and +0.26% relative to their baseline models). A subsequent four-window analysis spanning 2014–2025, with 116 catchments meeting record-quality criteria and 103 meeting common Lycaenidae support, did not support incremental prediction by preceding host-resource change or concordance among host changes. The association between early river ecological quality and subsequent potential host-network structure depended on the host catalogue. Reported upstream wastewater nutrient loads provided small additional predictive information for benthic ecological quality, but modelled wastewater geography could not be separated reliably from population concentration in the butterfly trait analysis. These results distinguish the predictability of assemblage states from that of assemblage change and show why spatial aquatic–terrestrial correspondence alone is insufficient evidence for temporal transmission of wastewater effects. Occurrence-based resource and consumer indices do not measure local feeding, abundance or material flux.

## Introduction: one question, three contrasts

1. **Exposure specificity.** Downstream-routed discharge is a useful environmental pressure to investigate because its source and receiving network are geographically defined. Distinguish directly reported annual facility loads, measured receiving-water nutrients, and modeled wastewater-associated geography. Show their limitations rather than treating them as one exposure.
2. **Spatial vs temporal.** Space-for-time substitution is an established question, not a new universal theory. Blois et al. (2013), [PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1220228110), evaluated correspondence under climate gradients; [Biological Reviews (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.13004) reviews assumptions across ecology and evolution. Our empirical contribution is the combination of repeated same-site river data, shared catchment trait comparisons and actual temporal holdout of terrestrial consumer indices.
3. **Resource correspondence vs transfer.** Allen et al. (2024), [Ecology Letters](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.14401), synthesized actual consumer allochthony in stream–riparian ecosystems and emphasized temporally uneven subsidy flows. This study does not measure allochthony. Rashid et al. (2026), [Diversity and Distributions](https://doi.org/10.1111/ddi.70245), assessed host-related constraints on European butterfly climatic niche margins. Our distinct question is the extra predictive information in host occurrence composition, and whether host changes predict subsequent butterfly recording changes, under fixed geographic folds and catalogue checks. Neither citation establishes that this combination is unprecedented.
4. **Hypotheses and history.** Report the original analyses as exploratory, the three-window forecast's pre-score intersection criterion as frozen, and the later four-window and matched-design tests as new retrospective endpoints on a previously inspected T3. The prior focus on Lycaenidae and all other butterflies is acknowledged; no outcome-blind selection of the focal family is claimed.

## Methods: keep four evidence units distinct

- River monitoring: EEA benthic EQR, reported annual facility N/P in 2018 and 2022; region holdouts, source selection and overlapping stations. Same-site French TP/I2M2 panel: 263 stations, 73 H7, 24 H6, 2018 and 2022. Measured TP is not sewage source attribution.
- Catchment trait data: 232 H7/90 H6, 454 original H7-period rows, 451 NUTRI-eligible. NUTRI is plant habitat-affinity score, HNS is score of known larval hosts, not leaf nutrient concentrations or measured interaction. France73 same support 146 rows. Additional population-controlled subset 437 rows, 225 H7/88 H6. Within-H6 genus decomposition uses separate 183 H7/40 H6 panel.
- Potential host graph: rarefied species inclusion from 20 butterfly and 50 plant indexed records. Candidate known host adjacency vs structured expected adjacency preserving plant family, record frequency, and geographic profile. Retain original two-period results and distinguish full-panel null from training-early H6-disjoint null. 46 species common to two catalogues in the cross-catalogue tests. Plant/consumer decomposition is exact algebra, not causal mediation.
- Temporal forecasting: 2014–16, 2017–19, 2020–22, 2023–25 indexed data, 202 minimum count and 116 all-window quality H7; 103 H7/53 H6 common high-quality Lycaenidae. Three-window frozen state forecast used 133 H7/67 H6 for common 46 species. Four-window forecast fits earlier plant changes to next butterfly changes and then scores fixed predictions in later interval. Same-design comparison uses same target, rows, historical folds, covariates and Ridge10 for state, signed change, both and null counterparts. The late fixed-consumer EQR test holds 2017–19 butterfly composition constant and evaluates H6 geographic generalization, not a sealed temporal holdout.
- Evaluation: report percentage MSE reductions relative to the explicitly named baseline, within each separate outcome and source. Positive values indicate predictive information, not a positive biological response or percent change in abundance. Distinguish coefficients, nominal fixed-error bootstrap intervals, corrected paired contrasts and STOPs. No pooled MSE meta-analysis, no p-value vote counting, no treating overlapping samples/catalogues as independent ecological studies.

## Discussion: ecological interpretation and boundary

The most useful distinction is between **resource-state association** and **resource-change predictability**. Persistently distinct habitats, species pools and recording practices can yield stable spatial correspondence without detectable change coupling over a particular interval. Resource availability could constrain establishment and maintenance, while shorter-term dynamics depend on climate, management, phenology, population processes or observation variation. These are candidate explanations, not identified mechanisms. State gain is small, stronger common-design sensitivity is not robust across catalogues, and absence of improvement is not an equivalence test or absence of ecological effect. Different scale and temporal aggregation can attenuate change signals.

The wastewater evidence is strongest within the river compartment and weaker for attribution across the water–land boundary. Specifically, directly reported loads and observed chemistry add predictive information to benthic models, while geographic proxies are sensitive to source routing, population and chemistry. The terrestrial trait relation and temporal consumer forecast do not identify the source of nutrients or observed cross-ecosystem subsidies. The common-catchment comparison should be the bridge, not a chain of coefficients multiplied across different data sets.

The main paper can be coherent as an observational catchment study of predictability and attribution limits; a positive wastewater-to-butterfly causal story is not supported. A separate interaction-focused manuscript is only justified if independent sampling or a stronger controlled state/change replication becomes available; do not split the same observations into two overlapping claims. The next genuine validation would be a source-specific exposure panel with matched time-varying nutrient measurements, near-river host observations and standardized butterfly surveys, or an untouched future time period; not further tuning the already opened 2023–25 sample.
